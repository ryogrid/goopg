# Design review — `08-target-design.md`, `09-verification-and-acceptance.md`, `TODO.md`, `README.md`

Reviewer: design/feasibility pass, 2026-09-02, against HEAD `372f9ded8`.
Inputs: the bundle, the prior-round inventory, the measured parity state, and
spot-verification against the tree (`internal/optimizer`, `internal/executor`,
`internal/testutil/estimateaudit`, `analysis/leftdeep-joins/`).

---

## Verdict

**Sound thesis, honest evidence base, and a phase plan whose two load-bearing
premises are contradicted by measurements that already exist in this
repository.** The bundle is the best-argued planner document this project has
produced: it correctly identifies that goopg's problem is *reach and inputs*
rather than missing machinery, it does not re-propose a single recorded no-go,
and 09 §1 is a genuinely excellent piece of gate design. But it should not be
executed as written. Three things must be fixed first:

1. **The claim that `GOOPG_PGSHAPED_COLLAPSE` gates join-order search "for a
   whole dialect of SQL" is false on the measured corpora.** A git-tracked
   production test in the same package
   (`internal/optimizer/collapse_corpus_test.go`) pins the flag's blast radius
   at **0 of 22 TPC-H queries and 2 of 99 TPC-DS queries (Q72, Q75)**. 07 §3.1's
   "the broader one is not the outer-join peel" is backwards, and TODO P3-00's
   "single largest change in what the search enumerates" is off by roughly two
   orders of magnitude in corpus terms.
2. **Phase 0's own instrument cannot produce a meaningful first number.** goopg
   emits no bare `Hash` node, so a normalised *tree* diff flags every hash join
   in every query; no TODO item creates the node; and A3 targets `MISSING-NODE
   = 0` with nothing scheduled to close it. Separately, P0-02 rewrites the
   EXPLAIN cost text that `estimate-audit` — the bundle's own "only committed
   goopg-vs-PG instrument" — parses, and nothing sequences that.
3. **The ordering argument in 08 §2.3 is built from measurements taken on a
   planner that has been deleted**, which is the exact error 07 §1 warns
   against. Round-4, the NDistinct-standalone regression and C4 are all
   pre-M0127; the integer DP whose weights they indict no longer exists, and the
   one *post*-M0127 datum about widening enumeration (the P5.9 `PGSHAPED_DP`
   flip: 0.982× total, worst query 1.36×) points the other way and is not cited.

None of this invalidates the design. Fix the premise, re-sequence P3-00 and the
Phase-2 plumbing, split the seven items that are milestones wearing checkboxes,
and turn B1–B4 into a programme register rather than bars, and this is
executable.

## The phase-ordering question (review item 2)

### What 08 §2.3 argues

Instruments → statistics → cost inputs → search coverage → upper planner →
parallelism → consolidation, on the ground that "a wider search space is not
safe until the numbers that rank it are right", derived from Round-4 (ANALYZE
fixed Q5 23×, broke five queries) as the symmetric statement of *"statistics are
not safe to keep on until the planner can cost the join orders they enable."*

### Stress test

**The argument is sound in principle and its evidence has expired.** Every
citation supporting it — Round-4, the NDistinct standalone regression, C4
binary-only (risk R3) — was produced against the integer subset-bitmask DP that
M0127-P6.3 deleted, with cost weights that the record explicitly says were
*"calibrated to the saturated-NDistinct regime"*. Those weights no longer exist.
07 §1 warns the reader about exactly this class of stale citation and 08 §2.3
then commits it.

The one post-M0127 measurement of a search-space widening is the P5.9 flip of
`GOOPG_PGSHAPED_DP` — a corpus-wide change in what is enumerated, ranked by
today's cost model and today's statistics — and it produced 23 plan MATCHes,
**0.982× total** and a worst query of 1.36×. That is the opposite of an
aggregate surprise, and 08 does not cite it.

**The argument is also mis-applied at the item level.** §2.3's reasoning is
about a change that moves *many* estimates or *many* enumerations at once.
P3-01…P3-04 (outer joins into the DP) is such a change: it restructures the
search for most of TPC-DS. **P3-00 is not.** Its measured blast radius is two
queries (F1). A two-query change cannot produce "a failure arriving as an
aggregate surprise across two suites"; it produces at most two moved plans, both
of which the P0 instrument will name individually. The document has bundled a
one-line flag flip and a jointree rewrite under one ordering argument because
they share a phase number.

### The case for leaving P3-00 in Phase 3

- Q72's problem is *ranking-sensitive*: flattening its search lets the DP choose
  among orders it has never had to price, using `calcJoinrelSize` which (P1-18)
  has **no join-type branch at all**. Q72 is precisely a mixed comma + `LEFT
  JOIN` shape, so the sizer that will rank its new candidates is the one Phase 1
  fixes. There is a genuine argument that flipping first measures the wrong
  thing.
- Q75 is the other eligible query and is CTE-heavy; CTE outputs have no
  statistics (ledger 944, and F28 shows the bundle has no item for it). Its new
  candidates would be ranked from a body-count fallback.
- One-variable-per-commit is better served by not interleaving phases.

### The case for moving it to immediately after Phase 0

- **It is the ideal first live test of the P0 instrument.** P0's exit is
  `changed=0` — a *negative* control. A negative control proves the harness does
  not lie in one direction only. P3-00 is a change with a **predicted, tiny,
  named** effect (Q72 and Q75 change; the other 119 queries do not), which is
  the strongest possible positive control for a differ that has never run. If
  the instrument reports movement on a third query, the instrument is wrong; if
  it reports none, the flag is still dead. Nothing else in the bundle offers
  that.
- **It closes an invalid negative result that is currently load-bearing.** The
  recorded NO-GO (F2) sits in the project's history as "collapse was measured and
  rejected". Leaving it unresolved until Phase 3 means months of planning around
  a verdict everyone believes and nobody can defend.
- **Q72 is the largest TPC-DS outlier (82×) and 07 says it is unreachable at any
  cost setting.** Removing or confirming the largest outlier *before* Phase 1
  makes Phase 1's signal cleaner, not dirtier: every P1 item is judged on suite
  totals that Q72 currently dominates.
- **Cost and reversibility are near zero.** One flag, one commit, `git revert`.
  The reproduction harness already exists
  (`analysis/leftdeep-joins/p59m-collapse-probe.sh`, with `GOOPG_PGSHAPED_DP_TRACE`
  adjudication).
- **It has no dependency on P1 or P2.** Nothing in the statistics or cost phases
  is a prerequisite; the only prerequisite is an instrument that can see the
  result, which is Phase 0.

### Recommendation

**Move P3-00 out of Phase 3 and run it as the last item of Phase 0, renamed
`P0-13`, explicitly framed as the plan-parity instrument's positive control.**
Concretely:

1. Land P0-01…P0-12 (with F3, F4, F19 folded in). Capture the baseline.
2. **P0-13**: flip `GOOPG_PGSHAPED_COLLAPSE` on, with the two eligibility tests
   quoted in the commit message as the pre-registered prediction ("exactly Q72
   and Q75 may move; TPC-H must be `changed=0`"). Run `PP` on both suites plus a
   timing arm on Q72/Q75 only — not a full both-suite timing arm, because the
   pre-registered blast radius is two queries and TPC-H is a proven control
   (`TestCollapseIsAControlOnTheTPCHCorpus`).
3. If Q72 improves or holds: keep the flip, and Phase 1 is measured on a corpus
   with a smaller outlier. If it regresses: revert, and you now own a *valid*
   negative result attributable to ranking — which becomes the first concrete
   target for P1-18 (jointype in `calcJoinrelSize`) and a citation §2.3 can
   legitimately use.

**Keep P3-01…P3-04 exactly where they are.** The Round-4 argument, weakened as
it is, genuinely applies to them: they change enumeration corpus-wide and their
new candidates are outer/semi/anti joinrels sized by the estimator P1-18 fixes.

**Two further ordering changes** (F20, F5):

- Move the **inert** half of Phase 2 (P2-01, P2-02, P2-02c, P2-03 — planner
  context and session GUC threading, no behaviour change) to the end of Phase 0.
  07 §3.2 argues for this itself. It makes every Phase-1 diagnosis a `SET`
  instead of a rebuild.
- Move **P4-01 (`PathTarget`)** and the range-table half of **P6-02** ahead of
  **P3-03/P3-04**, or state the layout contract for DP-built outer joinrels
  explicitly. Building nullable-side joinrels on `baseLeaf`/`baseOffset` is R5's
  bug class at its worst point.

Resulting order: **P0 (instruments + inert GUC plumbing + collapse control) →
P1 (statistics) → P2b (cost behaviour) → P4-01/range table → P3 (search
coverage) → P4 rest → P5 → P6 → P7.**

## The prohibitions (review item 8)

**As written they are aspirations for #1 and #3, and enforceable for #4, #5, #6.**

- **#1 "No query-specific forcing"** — undetectable by any gate in the bundle. A
  reviewer reading a diff can catch the crude form (a table name, a query
  fingerprint); nobody can catch the real form, which is a constant chosen
  because it made TPC-H Q9 faster. And because A1–A5 are measured on the same
  121 queries the work tunes against, the *acceptance criteria themselves reward
  overfitting*. Two mechanisms make it enforceable: **(a)** a held-out corpus
  never used to decide a change (JOB is the right choice for a join-order
  bundle), reported at every phase close; **(b)** an upstream-citation lint —
  every numeric constant in the cost and selectivity files carries a
  `postgres/<file>:<line>` citation, enforced by a meta-test. (b) converts "no
  query-specific forcing" from a taste judgement into a compile-time property:
  a constant with no upstream line has no defence.
- **#2 "No new penalty multiplier on cost totals"** — detectable by diff review
  today, and *mechanically* detectable with F19's cost golden tests: a penalty
  multiplier makes a golden test fail against the PG formula. Currently there is
  no such test, so #2 rests on reviewer attention.
- **#3 "No calibration constant introduced to fix one query"** — same as #1.
  `GOOPG_INDEX_PROBE_MULT` staying at 1.0 is checkable; a *new* constant is not,
  absent (b).
- **#4/#5** (`-count=1`, `--no-verify`) — enforceable; the hook exists.
- **#6** (ledger row per deferral) — enforceable by review, and the project has
  a track record of doing it.

One addition worth making: **a prohibition on deciding a bar after seeing its
number.** F9 (`work_mem`) and F10 (A1/A2) are both cases where the bundle will
be tempted to move a bar to fit a result. Pre-registration — writing the
decision rule into the TODO item before the arm runs — is the discipline this
project already applies to flag provenance (R5) and should apply to bars.

## What the bundle gets RIGHT

Discriminating praise only — these are the parts a rewrite must not lose.

1. **09 §1 is the best thing in the bundle.** Five real incidents, each with
   "what the gate said" against "what was true", each generating exactly one
   binding rule. R1 (a plan-shape change must be timed), R2 ("no plan changed"
   names a suite) and R3 (a census measures its labeller) are the three rules
   that would have prevented most of this project's expensive planner mistakes.
   Deriving rules from incidents rather than from principle is why they will be
   followed.
2. **07 §1's three warnings, and the fact that 07 then applies them to itself.**
   §2.2 re-opens `shape_mismatches = 46` and declares its own headline metric
   provisional; §3.11 retires three claims in the project's own record
   (Haas–Stokes, per-connection stats, multi-pair equality) *including one from
   its own deferral ledger*; §5.1 says q18's estimate error "is not a defect"
   because PG is wrong in the same direction. A gap analysis that argues against
   its own inherited premises is rare and is worth more than the analysis.
3. **The thesis is correct and is the opposite of the previous four rounds'.**
   "goopg's planner is not missing PostgreSQL's machinery… what it is missing is
   reach and inputs" is the right diagnosis, and it follows the record: every
   recorded win came from structure (search space, candidate set, missing
   statistic), never from a cost term. Principle P2 states this and the design
   obeys it.
4. **No recorded no-go is re-proposed.** I checked every phase against the
   rejected list — multi-way hash join as a plan node, runtime join fusion,
   cost-driven join order over the integer DP, standalone accurate NDistinct,
   threshold/penalty multipliers, shape preferences — and found none of them,
   including none wearing a different name. 08 §8's treatment of
   `sortPartialRootPays` is exemplary: it converts a hard-coded shape preference
   into a cost comparison while *keeping* the measurement that justified the
   preference, and pre-commits to accepting either outcome (09 §7.4 case 1).
   That is how a prohibition and a measured divergence are supposed to coexist.
5. **§5.4's "both must move in one commit"** for the bitmap pair. It is the
   correct response to R2's incident, it is specific about which commit, and it
   names the order (lossy pages first, then remove the double charge). Generalise
   the pattern (F33) rather than treating it as a bitmap special case.
6. **09 §6 (measurement methodology)** is unusually complete: the ±17 % band with
   its provenance, the ban on per-query thresholds tighter than 1.2×, the
   sweep-tail collapse with its 73× measured instance, the artifact header with
   a *generated* flag line, and `host-load:` existing because one set of numbers
   was voided by load. This is a research-grade methodology section.
7. **The per-category exit criteria in 09 §5** (`join-order` diffs strictly
   decrease; `aggregation-strategy` and `sort-strategy` diffs strictly decrease;
   `parallelism` diffs decrease with the serial arm unchanged) are the right
   shape of bar: monotone, per-phase, and independent of guessing a starting
   point. They are better than A1/A2 and should carry more of the acceptance
   weight.
8. **Phase 6 exists at all**, and its exit is byte-identical plans. Most refactor
   plans end at "the new thing works"; this one ends at "the old thing is gone
   and nothing changed", which is the only ending that removes the sibling-paths
   tax the bundle correctly identifies as a standing multiplier on every other
   phase's cost.
