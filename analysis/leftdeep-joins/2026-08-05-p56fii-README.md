## 1. What the task was, and what actually blocked it

P5.6-f made Q9's joinrel estimate exact (5 997 241, from 479 779 280) and Q9's
plan did not move by a single node. The reason, ledgered at the time: the legacy
join-order SEARCH never calls `estimateJoin`. `estimateJoinCost` (bushy.go) is a
second cardinality implementation, and its production branch — the integer DP —
took `ndv` to be the maximum NDistinct across EVERY column of the edge's two
tables, ignoring the join key.

That was true, and fixing it was not sufficient. Two further defects sat under
it, both found by instrumenting the DP rather than by reading it:

**(a) The join-edge coordinate space.** A `joinEdge`'s key expressions are
written in the query's GLOBAL FROM-list coordinates, not table-relative ones. On
Q5, `c_nationkey` arrives as `Index: 16` against an 8-column `customer` and
`s_nationkey` as `Index: 8` against a 7-column `supplier`. `accurateKeyDistinct`
indexed `Stats.Columns` with it, so it returned 0 for **every join key in the
query** and `sideKeyDistinct` quietly served the table-wide maximum instead —
the very fallback the accurate path exists to replace. Where the stale index
happened to land IN range it was worse than useless: Q5's `n_nationkey` is
`Index: 3`, and `nation`'s fourth column is `n_comment`, so the search priced
that join by a comment field's distinct count. `edgeColName` hid all of it,
because its `cr.Name` fallback returned the right NAME whenever the index was
out of range. This is the third instance of the coordinate-space failure the
P5.6-e-ii ledger row describes for `RightKey`.

**(b) The stadistinct sign convention.** `accurateKeyDistinct` multiplied
`NDistinctFrac × RowCount` whenever the fraction was positive. Since
P5.6-e-iii, `NDistinct` and `NDistinctFrac` are two renderings of ONE estimate
and `StaDistinct()` arbitrates between them with upstream's 10 %-of-rows rule
(`get_variable_numdistinct`, selfuncs.c). Multiplying unconditionally is exactly
the branch `StaDistinct()` exists to decide.

## 2. Why the fix could not be half of itself

The first attempt added ONLY the superkey/FK proof to the production branch and
left the no-proof case on the table-wide maximum. That is
`2026-08-05-p56fii-halfway.txt`, and it made things **worse**:

| Q5 joinrel | before | halfway | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lineitem ⋈ supplier` | 39 981 | 5 997 241 | truthful — `supplier`'s `s_suppkey` proven |
| `customer ⋈ supplier` (`c_nationkey = s_nationkey`) | 10 000 | 10 000 | unchanged — no key, so still the table-wide max 150 000, when the join key has 25 distinct values and the real answer is 60 000 000 |

The DP picked the 6 000×-underestimated cartesian product, and Q5 went from
65.9 s to over the 150 s audit timeout. A search compares estimates: making one
of them truthful while its competitor stays wrong is not half a fix.

The landed change therefore does three things together — one divisor for both
search modes (`graphJoinKeyDivisor` + the §4 per-clause product), name-based
column resolution, and `StaDistinct()` rendering — because none of them is
measurable, or safe, alone.
