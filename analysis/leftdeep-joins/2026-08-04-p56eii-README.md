## The rejected arm (`*-postfix.txt`), kept as evidence

The first cut of this task ALSO corrected the join-key ndistinct lookup: it
resolved both keys in the merged left‖right coordinate space they are written
in (`RightKey` had been resolved against the right child's own schema, so it
read out of range and the right side of every equi-join contributed nothing to
`max(nd)`), and it let both column lookups resolve THROUGH a join. Both are
what upstream's `examine_variable` does.

Measured, that arm made every joinrel it touched more accurate and the queries
above them far worse:

| query | joinrel | baseline | with the arm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q9 | d6, d5 (deepest) | 1.1× over, 7.6× under | **exact**, **exact** |
| Q9 | final | 124.7× over | 176 424× over |
| Q8 | d4 | 3.3× over | 343× over |
| Q8 | final | 1.9× under | 2 171× over |

Two pre-existing defects were cancelling the missing `nd`:

1. goopg's ANALYZE stores the SAMPLE's distinct count with no Haas–Stokes
   scale-up (upstream `compute_distinct_stats`, analyze.c), so a 1 500 000-row
   unique key reads as ≈ 30 000 and every join above it divides by a number
   50× too small;
2. the M0126-0010 cap that bounds a join at `max(|l|, |r|)` fires ONLY on the
   nd-unavailable path — so supplying `nd` also removes the bound that had been
   holding those estimates down (Q8 d4: 5 997 241 capped → 624 279 803).

So the coordinate correction is deferred to **P5.6-e-iii**, which de-saturates
ANALYZE first and lands the lookup with it. The rejected run is committed
because that conclusion is only defensible with the numbers in front of it.
