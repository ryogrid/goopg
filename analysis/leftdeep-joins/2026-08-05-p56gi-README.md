## What was carried, and what it actually turned out to be

The item was filed as "run the DS05 gate for P5.6-g *and* P5.6-g-iv", with the
hypothesis that **P5.6-g** would be the mover: TPC-DS, unlike TPC-H, has
nullable join keys, so `(1 - nullfrac1)` could shift estimates and therefore
plan shape where TPC-H showed nothing.

The last DS05 sweep was captured at `ce027cee` (P5.6-f). Between it and HEAD
there are **three** estimator commits, not two — `4b820ab8` (P5.6-f-ii) landed
after that sweep and was also never gated on TPC-DS:

| arm | commit | what it added |
|---|---|---|
| A | `ce027cee` | P5.6-f (last DS05 baseline) |
| B | `4b820ab8` | P5.6-f-ii — the join key the SEARCH was never reading (`graphJoinKeyDivisor`) |
| C | `8ce056ff` | P5.6-g — `eqjoinsel_semi` MCV arm + `(1 - nullfrac1)` (`8ce056ff` = g-iii, instrument-only, so C is P5.6-g's code) |
| D | `f8338a09` | P5.6-g-iv — `canonicalizeQual` |

## 2. Plan-shape blast radius: 75 of 99 plans moved, and it is not P5.6-g

`EXPLAIN` (no ANALYZE) over all 99 queries at each of the four arms, S-cold,
same data dir, one binary at a time (`2026-08-05-p56gi-capture.sh`, captures
`…-plans-{A,B,C,D}-*.txt`). Every statement is EXPLAIN-prefixed, so nothing is
executed for real — Q14/23/24/39 hold two statements each. Q36/Q70/Q86 fail to
parse in **both** arms; they are the known dsqgen artefacts that fail on PG too.

Noise floor first: the same binary captured twice produced **byte-identical**
plans for every query, so every diff below is a real, attributable change and
none of it is the plan-snapshot nondeterminism M0125-0047 records.

**The filed hypothesis is measured false.** P5.6-g — the MCV arm and
`(1 - nullfrac1)` — moves exactly ONE TPC-DS plan. Its estimates move; the
search's choice almost never does. The 74-query churn belongs to **P5.6-f-ii**,
which taught the join-order SEARCH to read the join key at all: before it the
integer DP priced an edge by the maximum NDistinct over every column of the two
tables, so essentially every multi-way join in the corpus was ordered on a
number unrelated to the key being joined on. That is the change this sweep
actually gated, and it had no gate of its own.

P5.6-g-iv's four are the OR-canonicalisation working as designed. Q41 is the
pure case — `(A AND X) OR (A AND Y)` becomes `A AND (X OR Y)` in the `item`
scan's filter, nothing else moves. Q13 is the load-bearing one: the three join
clauses repeated in all three OR arms (`ss_hdemo_sk = hd_demo_sk`,
`cd_demo_sk = ss_cdemo_sk`, `ss_addr_sk = ca_address_sk`) plus
`ca_country = 'United States'` are hoisted out, so goopg sees join clauses where
it previously saw an opaque OR and builds hash joins instead of a nested loop
carrying the whole disjunction as a filter — PG's own shape for Q13.

27 of the 99 queries contain an OR (against 2 in all of TPC-H), which is why
this sweep, not the TPC-H audit, was the place to measure `canonicalizeQual`.
Of those 27, four moved; the pass fires on the rest without changing what the
search picks.

## 3. What this leaves open

* **The gate has no plan-shape channel.** The four captures above were built by
  hand for this loop. A 74-query plan change passed the gate silently because
  the gate compares row counts and checksums only — which is the correct
  primary bar, but it means a search change of this size is invisible to it.
  Filed as a follow-up under M0127 rather than fixed here.
* **Q47 still times out** at 300 s in both arms (333 s → 328 s to first
  failure), unchanged by all three commits.
* Q13's new plan hashes all 1 920 800 `customer_demographics` rows. At SF0.5 it
  costs nothing (20 s → 21 s); at SF=1 that build side doubles, and no SF=1
  sweep has run since.
